Account structure

The map behind every financial report.

A clean Chart of Accounts gives every posting a stable home and keeps reports interpretable as the business grows.

Assets

Resources controlled by the business, such as cash, bank balances, receivables and fixed assets.

Liabilities

Amounts the business owes, including accounts payable and loans.

Equity

Owner funding, retained value and other ownership interests.

Income

Sales revenue and other income earned by the business.

Expenses

Operating costs, depreciation, bank fees, salaries and other expenses.

Control accounts

Accounts such as receivables, payables, inventory and tax connect detailed activity to the financial statements.

Designing your chart

Add detail only where it improves decisions.

A good chart is structured enough for reporting but not so fragmented that everyday posting becomes difficult.

01Start with defaultsUse the protected core accounts as the initial skeleton.
02Add meaningful detailCreate accounts such as Advertising, Vehicle Maintenance or Insurance when separate reporting is valuable.
03Use contacts for partiesKeep customer and supplier detail in subledgers instead of creating hundreds of ledger accounts.
04Use dimensions for analysisProjects, branches and cost centres can add management detail without multiplying ledger accounts.
05Review reportsRefine account structure when the Trial Balance and P&L no longer explain the business clearly.
How accounts become reports

The account type determines where balances are interpreted.

Posted movements are grouped by account type to build financial reports.

Trial Balance

Shows debit, credit and closing balances by account.

Profit & Loss

Uses income and expense accounts to calculate period performance.

Balance Sheet

Uses asset, liability and equity accounts as of a selected date.

General Ledger

Shows the detailed posted lines behind every account balance.

Dimension Profitability

Adds project, department, branch or cost-centre analysis to posted lines.

VAT Summary

Uses tax codes attached to commercial document lines rather than replacing the Chart of Accounts.

Questions

Practical questions about chart of accounts.

The answers below describe the current Triplem VIP Accounting workspace and its present boundaries.

What is a Chart of Accounts?

It is the organized list of ledger accounts used to classify the financial effect of transactions.

Can I add my own accounts?

Yes. The Accounting workspace supports custom accounts while protecting system defaults from ordinary deletion.

Why not create one account for every customer?

Customers and suppliers are handled through contact subledgers and document balances, while the Chart of Accounts remains the higher-level financial structure.

Can accounts be used for cash and bank activity?

Accounts can be marked and used as cash or bank accounts where supported by the Accounting workflow.
