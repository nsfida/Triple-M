Setup philosophy

Begin with the real financial structure, not a complicated template.

A maintainable accounting setup mirrors how money actually enters, leaves and moves through the business.

The simplest starting point is to create the wallets or accounts you actually use, choose the currencies they operate in and then begin recording the day-to-day transactions you already understand.

Inventory, loans, installments and assets can follow later when those workflows become relevant. Testing a representative set first is often more useful than importing a large history before the structure has been validated.

Setup questions

Keep the first configuration realistic and maintainable.

Good setup decisions are usually simple: mirror real accounts, test a small set, and avoid adding complexity before it has a purpose.

What should I create first?

Begin with the real wallets or accounts you use, choose the currencies they operate in, then record the categories and day-to-day entries you already understand.

Should I import everything before testing the workflow?

Usually it is safer to test a small representative set first. Confirm that balances, transaction history, documents and mobile use make sense before bringing in larger historical records.

What makes an accounting setup maintainable?

Clear names, consistent categories, realistic wallet structure and a routine for entering transactions promptly are more valuable than excessive complexity on day one.

Can I add inventory, assets or loans later?

Yes. Optional modules can be introduced as those records become relevant, allowing the core daily finance workflow to stay simple at the beginning.
