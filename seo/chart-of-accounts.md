## Build a Chart of Accounts that stays understandable

Organize the financial structure of the business without creating unnecessary complexity, then let journals, documents and reports reuse the same accounts.

## The map behind every financial report.

A clean Chart of Accounts gives every posting a stable home and keeps reports interpretable as the business grows.

### Assets

Resources controlled by the business, such as cash, bank balances, receivables and fixed assets.

### Liabilities

Amounts the business owes, including accounts payable and loans.

### Equity

Owner funding, retained value and other ownership interests.

### Income

Sales revenue and other income earned by the business.

### Expenses

Operating costs, depreciation, bank fees, salaries and other expenses.

### Control accounts

Accounts such as receivables, payables, inventory and tax connect detailed activity to the financial statements.

## Add detail only where it improves decisions.

A good chart is structured enough for reporting but not so fragmented that everyday posting becomes difficult.

## The account type determines where balances are interpreted.

Posted movements are grouped by account type to build financial reports.

### Trial Balance

Shows debit, credit and closing balances by account.

### Profit & Loss

Uses income and expense accounts to calculate period performance.

### Balance Sheet

Uses asset, liability and equity accounts as of a selected date.

### General Ledger

Shows the detailed posted lines behind every account balance.

### Dimension Profitability

Adds project, department, branch or cost-centre analysis to posted lines.

### VAT Summary

Uses tax codes attached to commercial document lines rather than replacing the Chart of Accounts.
